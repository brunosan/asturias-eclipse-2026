# Council review

This is a **simulated advisory council of seven explicit personas**, not a claim
that seven outside experts were contacted. Each persona was given the same
astronomy, terrain, cloud, access and traffic evidence and asked to review it
independently before a structured debate.

## Independent reviews

### 1. Inés Vega — eclipse geometry and terrain

“The starting premise needs correction: totality is not on the horizon. Near
Soto it occurs around 20:27 CEST with the Sun about 10.5° high at true azimuth
280.5°. Los Quebrantos, Cabo Vidio, Caroyas, El Espartal and Casa El Puerto all
retain strong terrain clearance; several famous Picos viewpoints do not. Do not
trade a safe open horizon for a few seconds closer to the centreline.”

### 2. Marta Nubes — mountain meteorology and satellite nowcasting

“Historical data favor the León/Astorga plain, but climatology is not the
12-August forecast. Elevation only helps a shallow low deck when the cloud
**top** is below the observer and the middle/high layers are clear; a reported
ceiling gives only the base. The CERRA samples actively argue against assuming
that a high Cantabrian ridge is clearer in late afternoon.”

### 3. Luis Tráfico — civil protection and road operations

“A free-flow route is a geometric baseline, not a journey-time estimate during
an exceptional event. DGT expects large movements and concentrated departure,
Asturias is operating 78 recommended sites, and final controls are still being
published. The map must force early branch decisions: León by midday, coast or
height by early afternoon, then no regional movement.”

### 4. Ana Parque — protected land, fire and access

“The most attractive mountain and headland sites are also the easiest to
overrun. Cabo Peñas, Farrapona, Sotres, Lagos and improvised rural León parking
cannot be advertised as ordinary drive-up fallbacks. A forecast advantage does
not override a closed road, shuttle-only plan, fire-risk restriction or the
need to preserve emergency access.”

### 5. Irene Casa — group logistics and human factors

“The best plan is the one the group can execute under uncertainty without
fatigue, arguments or a final panic drive. Walking or shuttling to Los
Quebrantos dominates if the coast is viable. If the south is chosen, sleep
there or leave early; a two-hour nominal drive plus an unknown queue is not a
credible group contingency.”

### 6. Tomás Duda — red-team and uncertainty

“The map should not turn sparse inputs into false confidence. The official
Asturias feed has no populated parking fields, the low-cloud conditional sample
has only 35 cases, and the current two-week forecasts are unstable. Keep
‘recommended,’ ‘conditional’ and ‘rejected’ visible; publish the raw samples,
model labels and open blockers.”

### 7. Elena Rescate — emergency medicine and observation safety

“Stopping movement early is a health intervention: it reduces crash exposure,
heat stress, missed medication and separation risk. Every branch needs water,
charged phones, offline maps, layers for the evening, a meeting point and
certified eclipse glasses. Filters come off only during complete totality and
go back on as soon as the bright solar surface returns.”

## Roundtable

**Vega:** “If low stratus is confirmed, Farrapona has the highest optical
position in the shortlist and still clears the western terrain by more than
6°.”

**Nubes:** “Only if the deck top is actually below roughly 1500 m and the upper
layers are clear. Our historical ridge samples do not support ‘higher is
automatically clearer,’ and afternoon mountain cloud can increase.”

**Tráfico:** “Even if the atmosphere is favorable, Farrapona is a dead-end
97-minute free-flow route with unresolved late shuttle details. It can be a
pre-positioned destination, not a reactive branch.”

**Parque:** “Agreed. The same distinction removes Sotres, Cabo Peñas and
improvised tracks from fallback status. Capacity and legal access are hard
gates.”

---

**Casa:** “Then the group plan should default to Los Quebrantos and contain
only one movement.”

**Duda:** “Yes, but ‘stay local’ must not become inertia if a persistent
north-side stratus regime is obvious by the morning.”

**Nubes:** “Resolve that with a clock: cross south by 11:00–12:00 if forecast
profiles, satellite and surface observations agree. After that, remove León
from the choice set.”

**Tráfico:** “And make the south destination legal before the day. Villadangos
is presently a concept, not a parking instruction.”

---

**Vega:** “For a local coastal split, Vidio and Espartal give better operational
geometry than chasing an iconic headland.”

**Tráfico:** “Direction must be selected once. A west-to-east reversal through
the A-8 corridor is exactly the behavior the map should prevent.”

**Duda:** “Require a broad, stable gap in three satellite frames. Do not react
to one model pixel or one webcam.”

**Casa:** “And be parked or on the shuttle by 16:00 even if the cloud edge still
looks tempting.”

---

**Rescate:** “What happens if the final cloud edge moves after 16:00?”

**Nubes:** “No forecast system can remove that residual risk.”

**Parque:** “The answer cannot be an illegal or unsafe relocation.”

**Council:** “Accept the uncertainty, make only a safe walk-scale adjustment
inside the managed area, and preserve the experience rather than converting it
into a road emergency.”

## Verdict

### Consensus

- **Default:** Los Quebrantos by foot/shuttle if the coast and up-sun corridor
  are viable.
- **Coastal branch:** Vidio/Caroyas west or Espartal/Xagó east; choose one
  direction by 14:00 and stop.
- **Low-cloud branch:** use a measured height ladder, led by Casa El Puerto.
  Require cloud top at least 200 m below the site and clear middle/high layers.
- **South branch:** decide by 11:00–12:00 and use a pre-confirmed legal or
  transit-accessed location. It is not an afternoon fallback.
- **Movement stop:** be parked, walking or on the shuttle by 16:00; no regional
  relocation after 18:00.
- **No false traffic arithmetic:** municipal population and people capacity do
  not yield reliable queue time or parking supply.

### Material tensions

- Farrapona is optically attractive but operationally fragile.
- The León plain has a much better historical cloud regime, but the crossing
  and destination must be chosen before the reliable nowcast window is fully
  mature.
- A narrow coastal gap can look tempting, but its forecast lifetime may be
  shorter than the traffic delay.

### Recommended actions

1. Scout the three local sites and one high-ground site before 8 August.
2. Obtain a real south-side destination or remove “Villadangos” from the
   executable plan.
3. Close every open access, shuttle, fire and roadworks blocker on 11 August.
4. Name one decision-maker and use the time gates in
   `docs/DECISION-PLAYBOOK.md`.
5. Keep the evidence tables available offline.

### Risk flags

- final municipal/DGT controls not yet published;
- no official parking data for the Asturias sites;
- AP-66 Negrón and eastern A-8/A-64 works;
- protected-area and fire-risk restrictions;
- small conditional meteorology samples;
- exact Coto Bello and Cuitunigru viewing coordinates unresolved;
- crowd departure after totality in dusk.

### Dissent retained

Vega would rank Farrapona first **if** the group is already pre-positioned, the
official eclipse shuttle/return is confirmed, and direct observations prove it
is above a shallow deck. The operational majority refuses to present it as a
day-of fallback from Soto. The atlas preserves that dissent by marking it
“pre-position only,” not deleting it.
