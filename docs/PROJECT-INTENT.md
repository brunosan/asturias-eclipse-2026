# Project intent

## Purpose

The 12 August 2026 total solar eclipse reaches totality over Asturias late in
the day. A useful viewing decision must combine solar geometry, terrain,
several cloud layers, road and shuttle constraints, crowd pressure, protected
land, and the option to cross the Cantabrian divide.

This project turns those inputs into a small set of explicit, auditable
branches. Soto del Barco town centre is used only as a public routing reference
for distance and travel baselines.

## Intended outcome

The interface should help an observer:

- understand that totality is about 10° high at azimuth 280–281°, rather than
  at the later sunset bearing;
- stay local when the nearby coast is viable;
- choose one coastal direction when a stable cloud boundary justifies it;
- distinguish cloud base from cloud top before considering elevation;
- cross toward León early, or remove that branch from the day plan;
- stop moving when access, weather, traffic, or timing evidence is inadequate.

The primary product is a decision process, not a single “best place.”

## Safety and evidence principles

- **Fail closed.** Missing or stale live data never becomes a positive signal.
- **Observable triggers.** Each branch names the weather, access, and timing
  facts that must be true before it is actionable.
- **One movement.** Regional chasing stops after the stated commitment gates.
- **Geometry before popularity.** An iconic viewpoint is not useful when
  terrain blocks the relevant azimuth or access cannot absorb demand.
- **Capacity is not parking.** Published people capacity and municipal
  population are never converted into invented queue times.
- **Provenance stays visible.** Calculated values link back to candidate inputs,
  derived rows, horizon profiles, source URLs, and validation checks.
- **Operational uncertainty is retained.** Conditional and rejected sites stay
  visible with the reason; dissent is not erased.

## Scope

Included:

- eclipse geometry and terrain screening;
- candidate-site assessment;
- historical cloud-layer sensitivity analysis;
- live Open-Meteo forecast retrieval;
- live DGT incident retrieval;
- official-source links for satellite, roads, mobility, fire, and access;
- scenario maps, decision gates, and a simulated advisory council.

Not included:

- a guarantee of clear sky;
- a credible eclipse-day traffic ETA;
- private-land access or parking permission;
- unattended automatic route selection;
- collection of personal location or identity data;
- publication of private prompts, transcripts, correspondence, or hosting
  metadata.

## Evidence cut and refresh policy

The research snapshot is dated 29 July 2026. Live widgets show their own
retrieval state. Access rules, official warnings, traffic orders, fire risk,
shuttles, and closures must be refreshed immediately before use.
